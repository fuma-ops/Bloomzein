# Title craft — high-search + curiosity

A Bloomzein title has two jobs, and they happen in order:

1. **Get found.** The title must be built on a phrase women *already search for*.
   That's what makes it high-SEO / high-search-volume: you're meeting existing
   demand, not creating it. If nobody searches the underlying question, the best
   writing in the world never gets opened.
2. **Get opened.** Once it's in front of her — in search results, in the Read
   grid, in a "Keep reading" card — it has to spark enough curiosity that
   scrolling past feels like leaving a question unanswered.

Miss job 1 and the article is invisible. Miss job 2 and it's visible but
ignored. You are always doing both.

## Step 1 — start from search demand, never from a clever idea

Before you write a single title, list the **real queries** a woman would type
around this topic. Think in her words, not editorial words:

- Symptom queries: "why am I so tired all the time", "why do I wake up at 3am",
  "why is my hair falling out", "bloated before period"
- Question queries: "how to stop overthinking", "how to fall asleep faster",
  "what causes anxiety", "is my period normal"
- "Why do I / why am I" queries — enormous volume, and they map perfectly onto
  Bloomzein's explain-your-body voice.
- Comparison / list queries: "signs of burnout", "foods for period cramps",
  "best sleep routine"

The winning topics sit where **high search volume** meets **something Bloomzein
can genuinely explain** (usually through the lens of hormones, cycle, sleep,
nourishment, or the nervous system). That intersection is the batch.

If you have web search available, sanity-check that the phrasing is one people
actually use (autocomplete-style, plain, often a full question). If you don't,
lean on the plainest, most literal way she'd ask — that's almost always the
higher-volume phrasing.

## Step 2 — keep the searchable phrase *intact* in the title

The single most common mistake is burying the keyword under a metaphor. The
searchable phrase should survive, close to verbatim, at or near the front of the
title so it matches the query and she recognises her own question:

- Search: "why am I so tired all the time" →
  **"Why Am I So Tired All the Time?"** (keep it) — not "The Quiet Thief of Your
  Afternoons" (beautiful, unsearchable, unrecognisable).
- Search: "anxiety before period" →
  **"Why You Feel More Anxious Before Your Period"** — not "The Storm Before the
  Bleed".

You earn the artistry in the *dek* (the italic subtitle under the headline) and
in the body, where SEO no longer constrains you. The title carries the keyword;
the dek carries the poetry.

## Step 3 — shape it into one of the patterns that convert

These patterns rank *and* pull. Pick the one that fits the query:

- **The recognised question** — mirror the search verbatim.
  "Why Do I Feel Anxious for No Reason?" · "How Do I Stop Overthinking?"
- **"Why You / Why Your ___"** — Bloomzein's signature. Implies a hidden cause
  she'll want revealed. "Why You Keep Waking Up at 3 A.M."
- **"What Your ___ Is Trying to Tell You"** — reframes a symptom as a message.
  "What Your Cravings Are Trying to Tell You" · "What Your Skin Is Telling You"
- **"The Real Reason ___"** — promises the answer under the obvious answers.
  "The Real Reason You're Always Exhausted"
- **"Signs of ___ / Signs You're ___"** — high-volume list intent.
  "The Signs You're Burnt Out (and Don't Realize It)"
- **"How to ___"** — direct how-to demand. Keep the verb she searched.
  "How to Fall Asleep When Your Mind Won't Stop"
- **The gentle contradiction** — challenges a belief she holds.
  "You Sleep 8 Hours — So Why Are You Still Exhausted?"

Numbers, "(and why it happens)", and question marks all lift curiosity when they
fit — but never at the cost of dropping the searchable phrase.

## Step 4 — tie the phase-tagged ones to cycle searches

Bloomzein's edge is the menstrual cycle. Whenever a topic has a cyclical angle,
prefer the phrasing that captures the *very high-volume* "X before period" /
"X during period" / "period X" searches, and tag the article's `phase`:

- "Why You Feel More Anxious Before Your Period" (luteal)
- "Why You Sleep Worse the Week Before Your Period" (luteal)
- "Why Your Skin Breaks Out Before Your Period" (luteal)

These win twice: they hit a big search term *and* they're uniquely on-brand.

## Worked examples (search → title → dek)

**Example 1**
Search: "why am i so tired all the time" (very high volume)
Title: **"Why Am I So Tired All the Time?"** (keyword kept, question form)
Dek: *You sleep, you rest, you do everything right — and still the tiredness
follows you around. When fatigue won't lift, your body is usually pointing at
something.*

**Example 2**
Search: "how to stop overthinking"
Title: **"How to Stop Overthinking Everything"** (keyword kept, "everything"
widens curiosity)
Dek: *The 2 a.m. replays, the imagined conversations, the decisions you make and
un-make. Overthinking isn't a character flaw — it's a loop, and loops can be
broken.*

**Example 3**
Search: "anxiety before period"
Title: **"Why You Feel More Anxious Before Your Period"** (phase: luteal)
Dek: *Same week, every month — the dread, the tears, the racing mind. It isn't
you falling apart. It's your hormones, and there's a pattern you can learn.*

## Pitfalls

- **Poetry in the title slot.** If the keyword isn't recognisable, move the
  poetry to the dek and put the search phrase back.
- **Made-up demand.** "The Art of Soft Mornings" reads lovely and is searched by
  almost no one. Start from a query, always.
- **Keyword with no curiosity.** "Sleep Tips" is searchable but flat. Add the
  hook: "The Sleep Tip Most Women Skip."
- **Two ideas fighting.** One title, one promise. If you're cramming two hooks,
  split it into two articles.
- **Clickbait that the body can't pay off.** The article must fully deliver the
  title's promise, or she bounces and trust erodes. Curiosity is a contract.
